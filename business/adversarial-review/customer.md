# Customer: Explain why you would or wouldn't pay for this

> **Role brief:** speak as the actual buyers. Four voices, because a school is not one
> customer — it is four people with different fears and different veto powers.
> Unfiltered.

---

## Voice 1 — Mrs. Adeyemi, Proprietor & Head Teacher
### 380 students · ₦95,000/term fees · owns the school outright

### Why I would pay

**Because you named my worst fortnight of the year and you didn't need me to explain it.**

Every term I sit in this office until 10pm for two weeks. Twenty-two teachers, and I
chase eleven of them for score sheets. Then my admin types everything into Excel, and
every single term there is a mistake — last term a JSS2 girl's total was wrong and her
mother came here and shouted at me in front of other parents. **In front of other
parents.** That is what I am buying protection from. Not software.

**₦15,000 is not money to me.** I collect ₦36 million a term. ₦15,000 is one student's
fee for a term — actually less. If you genuinely save my admin two weeks of work and
stop one angry mother, I have made a profit on you in the first month.

**Three things you said that made me listen:**

1. **The airplane mode thing.** I've had two of these systems demonstrated to me. Both
   times my teachers said *"madam, the network here is bad, it won't work."* And they
   were right, and we went back to paper, and I had wasted money. You turned off your
   internet and it still worked. **That is the only reason I let you finish talking.**

2. **You are not charging me in August.** The last company wanted ₦12,000 every month.
   Twelve months. School is closed in August — why am I paying you in August? Nobody is
   here. You understood that without me saying it.

3. **The website.** I got a quote for ₦180,000 for a website. You are giving it to me
   inside ₦15,000 a term. My competitor down the road has a Facebook page only. When
   parents search for schools now, they search on their phone. That matters more every
   year.

### Why I might not pay

**I have been disappointed before.** Two vendors. Both charming, both had beautiful
demos, both vanished. One of them still has my student data and doesn't answer my calls.
**So when you say "your data is always yours" — show me the button. Right now, on your
phone.** If you can't show me, you are the third one.

**I am worried you are one person.** You came alone. You will set it up yourself, train
my teachers yourself, answer my WhatsApp yourself. What happens when you get a big
contract in Lagos and I'm a ₦15,000 customer? What happens if you get sick during exams?
**My term cannot depend on one man's health.**

**And I looked at your website.** You say "20+ Active Schools." Fine — **give me two
names and let me call them.** I don't want a reference you chose. I want to call a
principal like me and ask if her teachers really use it. If you cannot give me two
names, then that number is not true, and if that number is not true I don't know what
else isn't.

`[The audit anticipated exactly this — see 02-product-claims-audit.md C3.]`

**Your programmes page confused me.** You describe coding classes, competitions,
scholarships — as though they exist. So I asked: *"When is the next competition?"* And
you said you're building it. Then **why is it written in the present tense?** If the
coding programme is the reason I should choose you over the cheaper option, and the
coding programme doesn't exist, then what am I actually buying?

`[Audit H1.]`

### What would close me today

- Two reference principals I can call unannounced
- The export button, demonstrated on your phone, in this office
- One class set up before you leave, with my own JSS1 students in it
- A written page saying: what I pay, what I get, how I leave, and who owns my data
- **A trial that spans a full assessment cycle**, so I can see one complete CA-to-report-card round before paying anything

> **My verdict: I would pay ₦15,000/term. It is cheap and my pain is real. But I would
> pay it as an experiment, not a commitment — and I would tell exactly two people about
> you, and only after my first successful results week.**

## Voice 2 — Mr. Okonkwo, Principal (salaried, does not own the school)
### 620 students · reports to a proprietor board

### Why I would push for this

**Because results week is my performance review.** If report cards are late or wrong, it
is my name. The proprietor doesn't see the 200 hours; she sees the complaint. Anything
that reduces the chance of a public error protects my job.

And **it makes me look modern to the board.** I can walk into the board meeting and say
we digitised results processing. That is a good sentence for my career.

### Why I would quietly kill it

**Because my teachers will make my life hell.** Twenty-two teachers, and about eight of
them will resist. Not because it's hard — because it's *new*, and because writing scores
on paper means nobody can see when they entered them. **Your system timestamps
everything.** Some of my teachers will understand immediately that this makes them
visible, and they will not like it.

You should know this: **the person who has to force adoption is me**, and I get no
reward for it. If a teacher refuses and I have to escalate to the proprietor, I have
created a problem for myself. So if the training isn't done for me — if I have to become
your salesman inside my own staff room — **I will let it die quietly and tell the
proprietor it "didn't work with our teachers."**

**Second issue: I don't control the money.** I cannot approve ₦15,000. I have to ask.
And if I champion this and it fails, that is on me. **You are asking me to take career
risk for a ₦15,000 product.** Reduce my risk or you lose me.

### What would win me

- **You train my teachers, personally, in my staff room, at no cost to my time**
- Make it work well enough that my teachers *prefer* it — the phone entry actually helps here, most of them would rather type on WhatsApp than fill a paper sheet
- Give me a one-page printout showing hours saved that **I can hand to my proprietor with my name on it**
- Never make me the enforcer alone; come back mid-term and check in

> **My verdict: I want this, but I need you to remove the career risk. Do the training
> yourself, give me a document that makes me look good, and I become your internal
> advocate for free.**

## Voice 3 — Mrs. Bello, Bursar
### The gatekeeper nobody in this plan is talking to

### Why I don't care about your product

**Because it doesn't touch my problem.**

My problem is this: 620 students, and at any moment about 180 owe part of their fees. I
track it in a ledger and a spreadsheet. Every term there is a gap I cannot explain.
Parents claim they paid; I can't find the transfer. I chase defaulters by hand.

**You are selling report cards. I am drowning in receivables.** `[Confirmed gap — there
is no fee-management module.]`

So when the proprietor asks me *"should we pay for this?"*, my honest answer is: *"It's
₦15,000, it won't help me, and there's a bank agent who says he'll give us a whole system
free if we collect fees through them."*

**Understand what that means.** The Moniepoint agent came here last month. He offered
fee collection, automatic reconciliation, SMS to parents about outstanding fees, **and he
said the school management software is free.** Free. And it solves *my* problem, not the
principal's.

If I have to choose between paying ₦15,000 for something that helps Mr. Okonkwo, and
₦0 for something that helps me — **I am not going to fight for you.**

`[This is the payments-company threat from 03-market-analysis.md §4, in the voice of the
person who will actually make it happen.]`

### What would make me neutral, or even helpful

- Don't try to become my payment system — I don't trust a new company with fee money
- But **do** let me record who paid, print an outstanding-fees list, and stop the manual reconciliation
- **Or at minimum, integrate with whatever we already use** so I'm not entering data twice
- Tell me clearly that this doesn't create work for me

> **My verdict: today I am indifferent-to-negative, and I am the one who says yes to
> spending. Until you touch fees, someone who does will eventually walk in here and take
> the whole school — including the part you built.**

## Voice 4 — Mr. Yusuf, Mathematics Teacher
### The veto

### Why I'd use it

Honestly? **Because the paper score sheets are worse.** I teach five classes. Every term
I get five sheets, I fill them by hand, I add up 200 totals with a calculator, and if I
make one mistake I redo the column. Then I walk to the office and the sheet gets lost and
I redo it.

If I can enter scores on my phone while I'm on the bus home — **and you showed me it
works with no network, which is the only reason I believe you** — that is genuinely
better for me. Not for the school. For me.

### Why I'd sabotage it

**Because of the timestamps.** Right now, nobody knows I entered my scores at 11pm the
night before they were due. Your system knows. My principal can see it. That is a change
in my working conditions and nobody asked me.

Also: **it's my data plan.** You're asking me to use my own phone and my own data for
school work. It's small, but it's the principle. Nobody is paying me more for this.

And if it's confusing even once — if I lose scores I already typed — **I will tell every
teacher in the staff room that it doesn't work, and that will be the end of it.** We talk.
The staff room decides these things, not the principal.

> **My verdict: I'll use it if it's genuinely easier than paper and never loses my work.
> I will kill it if it embarrasses me once. And you have no idea how much power I have
> here.**

## Synthesis: what customers actually told us

| Finding | Implication |
|---|---|
| **The pain is real and the price is trivially affordable** | Software price is not the obstacle. Stop worrying about ₦15,000. |
| **The offline demo is the only thing that gets past prior disappointment** | Lead with it everywhere ([01](../01-value-proposition.md) §4) |
| **Unverifiable claims are actively costing trust** | Mrs. Adeyemi asked for two references. Fix [02](../02-product-claims-audit.md) C3 now. |
| **Programmes described in present tense create suspicion** | If the differentiator is fiction, the whole pitch is suspect (H1) |
| **The export button is a closing tool, not a compliance chore** | Build it and demo it live (C2) |
| **Solo-founder risk is a real, stated objection** | Answer it: written runbook, backups, export, and a named continuity plan |
| 🔴 **The bursar is indifferent and the fintech agent is already in the building** | This is the most alarming finding in the whole review. The gatekeeper's problem is unaddressed and a well-funded competitor solves it for free. |
| **The teacher holds a veto nobody is courting** | Free training, never lose data, and acknowledge the data-cost issue |
| **The principal needs career cover, not features** | Give him a one-page hours-saved report with his name on it |

### The single most important customer insight

> **Every voice said yes to the pain and yes to the price — but the person who controls
> spending (the bursar) said no, because her problem is fees, and someone else is
> offering to solve it for free.**

That is not a pricing problem or a product-quality problem. It is a **buyer-selection
problem**. The plan sells brilliantly to the principal and proprietor, and has no answer
for the bursar — who is the one the payments companies will call.

**Consequence for the plan:** fee tracking (Layer 4, [04](../04-business-model.md)) is
currently scheduled as a defensive nicety. The customer voices suggest it is closer to
**existential**, and should be brought forward — not to become a payment processor, but
simply to give the bursar a reason to want you in the building.
